Parastatals and Public Service Delivery

By Editorial Team — Published: 2026-08-05

Parastatals are government-owned agencies, commissions, or companies created to perform specialized functions. They are often established by law and may operate with varying degrees of operational autonomy. Examples include revenue agencies, regulatory commissions, and state-owned enterprises.

Because parastatals focus on specific operational tasks, they can deliver services more effectively than a large centralized ministry in some contexts. For example, a ports authority focuses on port operations, customs handles border revenue and clearance, and a national agency like NAFDAC ensures food and drug safety.

How They Work with Ministries

Ministries provide policy direction and oversight while parastatals execute specialized programs. Ministries set goals and budgets; parastatals implement projects and report on performance. A clear legal mandate and performance metrics help align their work with national priorities.

Examples of Public Impact

When well-managed, parastatals improve service delivery by focusing resources and technical expertise. Examples include improved port efficiency, faster passport issuance through dedicated agencies, and better public health outcomes when regulatory agencies ensure product safety.

Citizen Engagement

Citizens can engage with parastatals by using official customer service channels, filing complaints through published procedures, and participating in public consultations when available. Transparency—such as published reports, procurement notices, and contact points—strengthens accountability.
