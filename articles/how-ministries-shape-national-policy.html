How Ministries Shape National Policy

By Editorial Team — Published: 2026-08-05

Federal ministries are the primary engines through which national policies are made and implemented. Each ministry is assigned a policy area — such as health, education, finance, or transport — and is responsible for setting strategic direction, developing legislation or regulations, and coordinating with agencies and state governments for delivery.

Policy development typically begins with research and stakeholder consultations. Ministries commission studies, host roundtables with civil society and industry, and consult international best practices. The policy draft is then reviewed internally and, where required, submitted to the Office of the President or Cabinet for approval before being enacted or funded.

Once a policy is approved, ministries translate it into operational plans and budgets. This is where collaboration with parastatals and agencies becomes critical: while a ministry sets the policy, parastatals often execute it — for example, a ministry may set a national food security strategy while a specialized agency runs agricultural extension services.

Coordination and Oversight

Ministries also provide oversight to ensure that agencies follow agreed standards and deliver results. They publish annual reports, monitor program indicators, and may commission audits. Good governance practices such as transparency, clear performance targets, and public accountability improve outcomes and build public trust.

What Citizens Should Know

Citizens engaging with ministries should first identify the ministry responsible for their issue, review official guidance on the ministry website, and where appropriate use the ministry’s public contact channels for complaints or service requests. When making inquiries, providing clear information and any relevant documents speeds up responses.
