What is the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security?

The Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security (FMAFS) is the federal ministry responsible for agriculture and food-security policy and programmes. The Ministry's current website describes it as the successor to the former Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development (FMARD), with a focus on food security, agricultural sustainability, farmer empowerment and market access.

This page is an independent educational guide, not the official Ministry website. For current programmes, applications, requirements, announcements and government decisions, verify information through official FMAFS sources.

Official source: agriculture.gov.ng is the Ministry's current public website.

What the Ministry Does

The Ministry's work covers the national agricultural system, including food production and security, agricultural development, farmer support, market access, sustainable practices and coordination of programmes across the sector.

Its role is broader than farming alone. Agriculture policy also connects with food supply chains, agricultural inputs, finance, research, standards, trade, climate resilience and rural livelihoods.

Why the Ministry Matters

Agriculture supports food supply, employment, household livelihoods and economic activity. Federal policy therefore affects farmers, processors, agricultural businesses, consumers, researchers and other participants in the agri-food system.

Understanding which federal institution handles a particular agricultural issue can help citizens distinguish between policy and programme information from the Ministry and specialised services provided by agencies under or connected to the agricultural sector.

Core Areas of Responsibility

  • Agricultural policy and sector coordination.
  • Food-security programmes and planning.
  • Crop, livestock and fisheries-related agricultural development.
  • Farmer support, agricultural inputs and extension-related services.
  • Agricultural research, innovation and technology adoption.
  • Market access, value-chain development and agricultural investment.
  • Sustainable and climate-smart agricultural practices.
  • Coordination of federal agricultural programmes and partnerships.

Programmes and Current Areas of Work

The Ministry's public website provides information about programmes and initiatives across the agricultural sector. These include the National Fadama Coordination Office and the National Programme for Food Security, among other programmes and projects.

The Ministry also publishes research, reports, news and programme information. Programme names, eligibility rules and implementation arrangements can change, so citizens should check the current official programme page before applying or relying on an announcement.

Information for Farmers and Agricultural Businesses

People looking for agricultural support should first identify the type of service they need. Examples include farm inputs, fertilizer information, agricultural value-chain programmes, market access, quarantine requirements, research support and programme-specific assistance.

The National Fertilizer Management Platform, developed by the Farm Inputs Support Services Department of FMAFS, provides a digital platform for fertilizer-sector participants and farmers. Its published information includes registration, dealer information and support resources.

Specialised Institutions Connected to Agriculture

Not every agricultural service is handled directly by the Ministry. Specialised agencies perform particular regulatory or technical functions. For example, the Nigeria Agricultural Quarantine Service (NAQS) is a regulatory agency under FMAFS responsible for agricultural quarantine and sanitary and phytosanitary measures affecting plants, animals and aquatic resources and their products.

This distinction matters when seeking a service: the Ministry may provide policy and programme information while a specialised agency may handle a specific certificate, inspection, regulatory or technical service.

Who May Need the Ministry's Information?

  • Farmers and agricultural cooperatives.
  • Agribusinesses, processors and agricultural investors.
  • People seeking information about federal agricultural programmes.
  • Researchers, extension professionals and development organisations.
  • Citizens looking for food-security and agricultural policy information.
  • Businesses involved in agricultural inputs, production and value chains.

How to Find the Right Agricultural Service

  1. Define the service or problem, such as fertilizer, farm support, quarantine, livestock, fisheries or agricultural finance.
  2. Check the current FMAFS website for the relevant programme, department or agency.
  3. Follow the official service or application instructions rather than relying on third-party payment or registration links.
  4. Check requirements, deadlines and contact information immediately before applying.
  5. For regulated agricultural imports or exports, check the relevant specialised regulator, including NAQS where applicable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Was the Ministry previously called FMARD?

Yes. The current FMAFS website identifies the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security as formerly known as the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development (FMARD).

Does the Ministry provide every agricultural service directly?

No. The agricultural sector includes specialised agencies and programmes with distinct responsibilities. The correct authority depends on the service involved.

Where can I find current agricultural programmes?

Start with the Ministry's official website, where programmes, news, publications and resources are published. Always check the current eligibility and application instructions.

Where can farmers find fertilizer-related information?

The National Fertilizer Management Platform is a digital platform developed by FMAFS's Farm Inputs Support Services Department. Its published resources include fertilizer-sector registration and support information.

Is Nigerian Info affiliated with FMAFS?

No. Nigerian Info is an independent educational information website. Official government information should be verified through FMAFS and the relevant government agency.
