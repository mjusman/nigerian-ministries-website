What Is the Federal Ministry of Women Affairs?

The Federal Ministry of Women Affairs is a federal ministry that advises government on gender and children issues and on matters affecting persons with disabilities and older people. Its official mandate also includes initiating policy guidelines and coordinating gender equality and gender mainstreaming at national and international levels.

The ministry therefore has a policy, coordination, advocacy and social-development role. It is different from a general-purpose service centre: many services and programmes affecting women, children and other vulnerable groups are delivered through other federal institutions, state governments, local authorities and partner organisations.

Independent information: Nigerian Info is not the Federal Ministry of Women Affairs and is not affiliated with the Nigerian government. Use the ministry's official website and publications to confirm current programmes, contacts, eligibility rules and announcements.

Core Responsibilities

The ministry's published mandate includes responsibilities such as:

  • Developing and coordinating policies on women's development, gender equality and gender mainstreaming.
  • Advocating for women's rights and participation in social, economic and political development.
  • Supporting policy and public awareness relating to women, children and vulnerable groups.
  • Promoting women's access to education, information, skills and economic opportunities.
  • Addressing discriminatory social and cultural practices affecting women and girls.
  • Coordinating or supporting social-welfare activities and assistance for vulnerable groups.
  • Supporting the rights, development and protection of children, including coordination around relevant national frameworks.
  • Developing policies and strategies concerning persons with disabilities and supporting their integration and rehabilitation.
  • Conducting research, surveys, training and information activities on gender and social-development issues.
  • Working with national, regional and international institutions, civil society, development partners and other stakeholders.

These functions are drawn from the ministry's published mandate; the exact programmes and implementation arrangements can change over time.

Why the Ministry Matters

Women, children, older people and persons with disabilities can encounter barriers involving access to education, health, economic participation, protection and representation. The ministry's role is to help bring these concerns into national policy and development processes rather than treating them as isolated issues.

Its work also involves coordination. For example, a national policy may require cooperation between federal ministries, state institutions, regulatory or service agencies, civil society organisations and development partners. Understanding this division of responsibility helps citizens identify the institution that can actually handle a particular request.

Major Areas of Work

Gender equality and women's empowerment

The ministry formulates and coordinates policies intended to improve women's participation and opportunities in social, economic and political life and to mainstream gender considerations across government activity.

Children's welfare and protection

Child welfare and protection are part of the ministry's mandate. Its official publications also include policy material on child protection and related issues.

Persons with disabilities and older people

The ministry's mandate covers policy and social-development issues affecting persons with disabilities and the aged, including support for inclusion and, within its stated functions, rehabilitation and training activities.

Social welfare and vulnerable groups

The ministry describes responsibilities involving social-welfare coordination, support for vulnerable groups, research, training and public information.

Research, advocacy and partnerships

The ministry works with public institutions, non-governmental organisations, development partners, private-sector stakeholders and international organisations on issues within its mandate.

Departments and Internal Coordination

The ministry operates through departments and other internal structures that support policy, administration, social-development and programme responsibilities. Its official organisational information should be used when you need the current name or remit of a particular department.

For example, the ministry's published material identifies the Department of Special Duties in connection with its Anti-Corruption and Transparency Unit (ACTU). The ministry's organisational structure is available on its official website.

Programmes, Policies and Publications

Rather than treating programme names on third-party websites as permanent services, citizens should check the ministry's current announcements and publications. The official ministry site currently publishes materials on areas including child protection and social-development policy.

Recent official publications include policy briefs concerning ending violence against children and child-protection and welfare practices. These documents can be useful starting points for understanding the ministry's current policy work.

Information for Citizens

Who may need the ministry?

Citizens, civil-society organisations, researchers, development partners and public institutions may need the ministry for information about gender policy, women's development, child welfare and protection, disability inclusion, social welfare coordination, or related national programmes and policies.

Before contacting the ministry

  • Define the issue clearly and identify whether it is a federal policy matter, a direct service request, or a state/local matter.
  • Check the ministry's official website for current notices, publications, contacts and programme information.
  • Keep relevant documents and reference numbers available if your matter concerns an existing application or complaint.
  • For emergencies or immediate protection needs, use the appropriate emergency or law-enforcement and social-service channels rather than relying on an informational directory.

How to verify official information

Start with the ministry's official domain, womenaffairs.gov.ng. Confirm that a programme, contact detail, announcement or application procedure appears on an official page before relying on information shared through social media or third-party websites.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Federal Ministry of Women Affairs only for women?

No. Its published mandate also covers children, persons with disabilities and older people, as well as broader gender and social-development policy.

Does the ministry directly provide every service related to women and children?

No. The ministry has an important policy and coordination role, while specific services may be handled by other federal institutions, state governments, local authorities or partner organisations.

Where can I find the ministry's current programmes?

Check the ministry's official news and publications sections for current announcements and documents rather than relying on an old programme list on a third-party site.

Where can I find the ministry's organisational structure?

The ministry publishes an organisational structure on its official website. That source should be checked for the current department names and arrangements.
