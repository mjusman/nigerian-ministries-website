What is the Federal Ministry of Justice?

The Federal Ministry of Justice is the central legal institution of the Federal Government of Nigeria. Its official description says the Ministry facilitates a fair, impartial and efficient administration of justice and provides legal and related services to government institutions, citizens and residents.

The Ministry is headed by the Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice, with the Solicitor-General of the Federation and Permanent Secretary supporting the Ministry's work.

What the Ministry Does

The Ministry's published functions include representing the Federal Government in legal matters, providing legal advice to the President and government institutions, representing or supervising representation of the Federal Government in legal proceedings, supporting access to justice and due process, advising on treaty obligations, and providing ministerial oversight of allied justice-sector institutions.

It also interfaces with the legislative and judicial arms of government on matters within its mandate. This does not mean that the Ministry controls the courts; rather, its role includes legal representation, advice, prosecution and other functions assigned by law.

Core Responsibilities

  • Provide legal advice to the Federal Government and its institutions.
  • Represent or supervise representation of the Federal Government in legal proceedings.
  • Prosecute federal criminal matters through the appropriate prosecutorial structures.
  • Draft and vet Executive Bills and statutory instruments.
  • Support access to justice, citizens' rights and observance of due process.
  • Advise government on treaties and international legal obligations.
  • Coordinate or participate in justice-sector reforms and related interventions.
  • Provide law reporting and legal publications.
  • Exercise ministerial oversight over specified allied justice-sector institutions.

Legal Drafting and Government Legislation

The Legal Drafting Department states that it drafts and vets Executive Bills for presentation by the President to the National Assembly, drafts bills for the domestication of international conventions and treaties, prepares statutory instruments such as regulations and orders, and processes their publication in the Federal Gazette.

This helps explain the Ministry's role in the law-making process: the Ministry provides legal drafting and advice within the Executive branch, while legislation is considered through the constitutional legislative process.

Federal Criminal Prosecution

The Department of Public Prosecutions of the Federation is responsible for legal advice in federal criminal matters and prosecution of criminal cases on behalf of the Federal Government. Its published mandate also covers collaboration with relevant security and justice institutions on complex and specialised crimes.

Specific criminal investigations, prosecutions and court proceedings involve other institutions as provided by law. The Ministry should therefore not be treated as the universal point of contact for every criminal complaint in Nigeria.

Justice-Sector Institutions

The Ministry's official website identifies a number of allied institutions and parastatals under its oversight. These include the Nigerian Law Reform Commission, Legal Aid Council, Council of Legal Education, National Drug Law Enforcement Agency, National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons, Nigerian Institute of Advanced Legal Studies and Nigerian Copyright Commission, among others.

The precise responsibility for a service depends on the institution involved. Citizens should identify the relevant agency before submitting an application or complaint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Federal Ministry of Justice the same as the judiciary?

No. The Ministry is part of the Executive branch and performs legal, prosecutorial, advisory and related functions assigned by law. Courts exercise judicial functions independently within the constitutional framework.

Who heads the Ministry?

The Ministry is headed by the Attorney-General of the Federation and Minister of Justice. Current officeholders should be verified from the Ministry's official website because appointments can change.

Does the Ministry write every Nigerian law?

No. The Ministry has an important Executive legal-drafting role, including drafting and vetting Executive Bills and statutory instruments, but legislation follows the constitutional process involving the National Assembly and presidential assent where required.

Can the Ministry handle every legal problem faced by a citizen?

No. Many legal matters belong to state institutions, courts, regulatory agencies, law-enforcement bodies, Legal Aid Council or private legal practitioners, depending on the issue.

Where can I find official Nigerian laws?

The Federal Ministry of Justice provides a laws section and legal publications on its official website. Always check the official source for the current text and status of a law.

Is Nigerian Info affiliated with the Ministry?

No. Nigerian Info is an independent educational information website.
