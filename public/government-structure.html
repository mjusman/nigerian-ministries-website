The Federal Republic of Nigeria

Nigeria's federal system distributes public authority among the Executive, Legislature and Judiciary. This guide focuses on the federal level and explains where ministries, departments, agencies and other public institutions fit into that structure. It is an educational explanation, not an official government directory. For current institutional names, mandates and contacts, readers should verify information against the relevant official source.

1. The Executive Branch

The Executive branch is responsible for implementing and enforcing the laws written by the legislature. It is the branch responsible for the day-to-day administration of the country.

Key Components:

  • The President: The Head of State, Head of Government, and Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces. The President is elected for a four-year term and can serve a maximum of two terms.
  • The Vice President: Assists the President and acts in their place when necessary.
  • The Federal Executive Council (FEC): Also known as the Cabinet, it comprises the President, Vice President, and Ministers appointed by the President. Each Minister heads a Federal Ministry, which is responsible for a specific sector (e.g., Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Health).
  • Federal Ministries, Departments and Agencies (MDAs): These are public institutions with different mandates. Ministries provide policy and administrative coordination, while departments and agencies perform functions assigned by law or government instruments.

2. The Legislative Branch

The Legislature makes laws within its constitutional authority and performs oversight and other functions assigned by the Constitution. Nigeria's federal legislature is bicameral, consisting of the Senate and House of Representatives.

Key Components:

  • The National Assembly (NASS): The collective name for the two legislative houses.
  • The Senate (The Upper House): Comprises 109 senators, with three representing each of the 36 states and one for the Federal Capital Territory (FCT). The Senate is led by the Senate President.
  • The House of Representatives (The Lower House): Comprises 360 members, each representing a federal constituency based on population. It is led by the Speaker of the House.

The National Assembly makes federal laws and performs oversight and other constitutional functions. The Senate and House of Representatives work through committees and plenary proceedings; some presidential nominations require confirmation by the Senate. The National Assembly also considers federal public finance through the constitutional budget process.

3. The Judicial Branch

The Judiciary exercises judicial powers in accordance with the Constitution and applicable law. Courts hear disputes, interpret and apply law, and determine cases within their jurisdiction.

Key Components:

  • The Supreme Court: This is the highest court in Nigeria. Its decisions are final. It is headed by the Chief Justice of Nigeria.
  • The Court of Appeal: Hears appeals from lower courts.
  • The Federal High Court, State High Courts, Sharia Courts of Appeal, and Customary Courts of Appeal: These are courts of first instance and lower appellate courts with jurisdiction over various civil and criminal matters.

The relationship is more useful to understand as a system of different functions: the Legislature makes laws and exercises constitutional oversight; the Executive administers government and delivers services through ministries and other institutions; and the Judiciary adjudicates disputes and applies the law.

How Ministries and Agencies Fit In

Federal ministries and other public institutions are mainly encountered within the Executive side of federal administration. The Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation maintains a current list of federal ministries and links to many ministry websites. The exact organisation of institutions can change, so this page should be used as a conceptual guide rather than a permanent directory.

Ministry vs Agency: a simple distinction

A ministry is generally a major government administrative and policy institution headed by a minister. An agency, commission or other statutory body may have a more specialised mandate established by law or another government instrument. The exact legal status and relationship should be checked for the particular institution.

How Citizens Can Use This Structure

  1. Identify whether your issue concerns law-making, government administration, a public service, or a dispute.
  2. If it concerns a federal public service, identify the ministry or agency responsible for that service.
  3. Read the relevant institution page on Nigerian Info for context and related institutions.
  4. Follow the official institution website for current requirements, applications, notices and contacts.
  5. If the issue concerns a court matter or legal dispute, use the appropriate judicial or legal channel rather than treating an information directory as an official authority.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the three arms of government?

The federal government is commonly described through the Executive, Legislature and Judiciary, each exercising functions assigned by the Constitution and law.

Are ministries the same as agencies?

No. They can have different mandates, legal bases and administrative relationships. Check the particular institution's establishing law or official description when the distinction matters.

Where can I find current federal ministries?

The Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation publishes a current Ministries, Departments and Agencies list. Individual ministries should also be checked through their official websites.

Is Nigerian Info an official government website?

No. It is an independent educational resource. Official sources should be used for current government services, legal requirements and official notices.
