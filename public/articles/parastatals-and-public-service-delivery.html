Parastatals and Public Service Delivery

By Editorial Team — Published: 2026-08-05 — Updated: 2026-09-22

In Nigeria, the word parastatal is often used broadly for public bodies that perform specialised functions outside the ordinary ministerial departments. In current federal public-service terminology, you will also encounter MDA (Ministries, Departments and Agencies), commissions, authorities, corporations and other public institutions. The exact legal status of an institution depends on the law or instrument that establishes it. citeturn0search25turn0search10

Why the distinction matters: A ministry, agency or commission may have a different mandate, governing structure and service process. Knowing which institution has legal responsibility for an issue can save time and prevent a citizen from sending a request to the wrong office.

What is a parastatal or government agency?

There is no single operational model for every public institution. Some bodies regulate an industry; some provide a public service; some manage infrastructure; some collect revenue; and others perform specialised administrative or technical functions. Their powers and responsibilities come from their establishing laws, regulations and other official instruments.

The Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation maintains a current directory of federal ministries and agencies. The Bureau of Public Service Reforms also works on public-service reform and publishes material concerning ministries, departments and agencies. citeturn0search10turn0search1

How ministries and agencies can relate to each other

A ministry generally has a policy and administrative role within the federal executive structure, while a specialised agency may have a more specific statutory or operational mandate. The relationship is not identical across institutions, so it is safer to check the particular institution's establishing law and official organisational information rather than assume that every agency simply "implements ministry policy."

Government reform documents also emphasise the importance of aligning parastatals with their parent ministries or supervising bodies and clarifying the respective roles of ministers, permanent secretaries and agency leadership. citeturn0search29

Different kinds of public functions

  • Regulation: bodies such as NERC and NCC set or enforce sector rules within their legal mandates.
  • Revenue and administration: institutions such as the Nigeria Revenue Service and Nigeria Customs Service perform specialised revenue and border-related functions.
  • Identity and civil registration: NIMC and NPC perform specialised identity or population-related functions.
  • Infrastructure and transport: authorities such as NPA and NIMASA have specialised responsibilities in maritime and port administration.
  • Public safety and standards: agencies such as NAFDAC perform regulatory functions affecting products and public health.

These examples illustrate different institutional roles; they should not be read as meaning that each institution has identical powers, independence or reporting arrangements.

How public services reach citizens

For a citizen, the important question is often not the organisational label but which institution actually provides or regulates the service. A service may involve more than one public body. For example, an application may require identity verification, payment, regulatory approval or a separate supporting document handled by another institution.

Before starting a process, check the official website for the responsible institution, eligibility requirements, required documents, fees, application channel and complaint procedure. Avoid relying on screenshots or old social-media posts where the official source is available.

Service standards and complaints

SERVICOM was established as a mechanism for improving public service delivery across government Ministries, Departments and Agencies. Its published guidance states that citizens can complain when government service delivery fails, including through the relevant service window or SERVICOM channels. citeturn0search2turn0search30

This does not mean every complaint should bypass the institution involved. Start with the agency's published complaint or customer-service process when one exists, keep your reference number, and escalate through the appropriate route if the matter remains unresolved.

How citizens can use agency information responsibly

  1. Identify the service, regulation or public function involved.
  2. Find the responsible institution through its official website or a trusted government directory.
  3. Read the current service requirements before submitting documents or paying fees.
  4. Use only the official application or contact channel where one is provided.
  5. Keep receipts, reference numbers and correspondence.
  6. Use the institution's complaint route or SERVICOM where appropriate if service delivery fails.
