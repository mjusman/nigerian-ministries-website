Overview

The Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) is Nigeria's customs administration. Its statutory responsibilities include administering and enforcing customs and excise laws, collecting customs revenue, facilitating legitimate trade, preventing smuggling and customs-related violations, and working with other government institutions on border and trade matters.

The current legal framework is the Nigeria Customs Service Act 2023. The Act gives the Service a wider modern customs role that combines enforcement, revenue administration, trade facilitation, data and statistics, licensing and collaboration with other agencies.

Important: Nigerian Info is an independent educational website. It is not NCS and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Nigeria Customs Service. Customs duties, prohibited goods, import/export requirements and procedures can change, so traders should verify current requirements through official NCS channels.

What the Nigeria Customs Service Does

NCS's published mandate includes:

  • Checking travellers, baggage, cargo and mail within its customs responsibilities.
  • Assessing and collecting customs duties and other taxes assigned to the Service.
  • Enforcing import, export and excise restrictions and prohibitions.
  • Intercepting prohibited or contraband goods.
  • Protecting legitimate businesses from illegal trade practices.
  • Collecting customs and trade data for statistics, planning and policy purposes.
  • Using intelligence, risk assessment, examination, investigation and audit to enforce customs laws.
  • Working with other government agencies at ports, border stations and other approved locations.

Legal and Institutional Basis

The Nigeria Customs Service derives its current statutory mandate from the Nigeria Customs Service Act 2023. NCS states that the Act provides the legal foundation for its administration, management and enforcement of customs and excise laws.

The modern customs role extends beyond revenue collection. It includes trade facilitation, border-related enforcement, anti-smuggling work, trade statistics, licensing and cooperation with stakeholders and other public institutions.

Core Functions and Areas of Work

Revenue collection

NCS collects and accounts for customs duties, excise duties and other revenue assigned to it by law. Revenue administration is one part of its broader customs mandate.

Trade facilitation

NCS works to facilitate legitimate international trade while maintaining customs controls. Its official portal describes trade facilitation as a central part of its current operational focus.

Anti-smuggling and enforcement

The Service enforces customs laws and takes action against smuggling, customs fraud and other violations. Enforcement can involve intelligence, risk assessment, physical examination, investigations and audits.

Customs licensing and administration

NCS has statutory responsibilities relating to the licensing and registration of customs agents and other customs administrative processes.

Trade data and statistics

The Service collects customs and trade information that can support economic statistics, planning and policy administration.

Services and Digital Platforms

NCS currently provides or links to several public-facing services and platforms, including:

  • B'Odogwu / Nigeria Trade Portal for customs and trade-processing services.
  • Customs Verification Management System.
  • Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) programme and related applications.
  • Advance Ruling System for applicable customs classification, origin or valuation questions before importation.
  • Licences and permits administered through the customs framework.
  • Customs and trade information for importers, exporters and other stakeholders.

Digital services, eligibility, application procedures and requirements may change. Use the official NCS portal for the current process before making a payment or submitting business information.

Who Uses NCS Services?

  • Importers and exporters.
  • Licensed customs agents and logistics operators.
  • Manufacturers and businesses involved in international supply chains.
  • Authorised Economic Operators and companies seeking that status.
  • Travellers whose baggage or goods fall within customs requirements.
  • Government agencies and other institutions involved in trade, ports and border administration.

Requirements and Important Information

Customs requirements depend on the goods, transaction, tariff classification, origin, destination, importer/exporter status and applicable restrictions. There is no single checklist that applies to every import or export transaction.

Before moving goods, check the current tariff, prohibited/restricted-goods rules, documentation requirements, applicable duties and taxes, and the relevant NCS procedure. Traders should also use licensed customs professionals where required.

Be cautious of unofficial payment requests or claims that a third party can bypass customs procedures. Verify official payment instructions and service channels through NCS before transferring money or sharing sensitive commercial information.

Relationship to Government

NCS is a federal customs administration and works with other institutions involved in trade, ports, border management, security, finance and economic policy. Its responsibilities should not be confused with those of other agencies.

For example, customs revenue and customs enforcement are NCS responsibilities, while immigration control is handled by the Nigeria Immigration Service and monetary policy is handled by the Central Bank of Nigeria. Different agencies may operate at the same ports or border locations while retaining separate legal mandates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main role of the Nigeria Customs Service?

NCS administers and enforces customs and excise laws, collects assigned revenue, facilitates legitimate trade, combats smuggling and performs other statutory customs functions.

What law governs NCS?

The current statutory framework is the Nigeria Customs Service Act 2023.

Does NCS only collect import duties?

No. Revenue collection is important, but the Service also has responsibilities for trade facilitation, anti-smuggling, customs enforcement, trade statistics, licensing and other customs functions.

What is B'Odogwu?

B'Odogwu is the Nigeria Trade Portal described by NCS as a digital platform intended to simplify trade processing and provide access to approved customs service channels.

What is an Authorised Economic Operator?

The AEO programme is a compliance-based customs facilitation programme for qualifying operators. Current eligibility, application requirements and benefits should be checked on the official NCS platform.

Where can I check current customs requirements?

Use the official NCS website, the relevant customs service portal and current NCS rules and guidelines. Requirements can change, so an old social-media post or third-party checklist should not be treated as authoritative.
