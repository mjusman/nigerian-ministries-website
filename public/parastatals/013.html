What Is NERC?

The Nigerian Electricity Regulatory Commission (NERC) is an independent regulator of the Nigerian Electricity Supply Industry. It undertakes technical and economic regulation within the jurisdiction assigned to it by law.

NERC was established under the Electric Power Sector Reform Act 2005, which has been repealed and replaced by the Electricity Act 2023. The Commission's current role is therefore understood within the Electricity Act 2023 and related regulations.

Important: Nigerian Info is an independent educational website. It is not NERC and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Commission. Electricity tariffs, regulatory orders, licences, complaint procedures and other requirements can change, so verify current details on NERC's official website.

Core Functions

NERC's published role includes technical and economic regulation of the electricity supply industry. Its responsibilities include:

  • Licensing electricity operators within NERC's statutory jurisdiction.
  • Developing and enforcing electricity-sector rules, codes, standards and regulatory instruments.
  • Regulating applicable electricity tariffs and market arrangements.
  • Establishing customer rights and obligations and supporting consumer protection.
  • Monitoring licensee performance and compliance with regulatory requirements.
  • Providing regulatory oversight for electricity generation, transmission, system operation, trading and distribution activities that fall within its jurisdiction.
  • Supporting transparent, rule-based and consultative electricity-market regulation.

NERC and the Electricity Act 2023

The Electricity Act 2023 changed the structure of electricity regulation in Nigeria by allowing states to establish electricity markets and regulatory authorities subject to the Act. NERC continues to regulate activities within the federal jurisdiction, including inter-state and national-grid-related activities, and activities in states that have not assumed regulatory authority.

This means that not every electricity project or service in Nigeria necessarily falls under exactly the same regulator. Before applying for a licence or authorisation, applicants should check whether NERC or a relevant state electricity regulator has jurisdiction over the proposed activity.

NERC's published regulatory FAQs explain this decentralised framework and identify the types of licences and approvals it continues to issue where it has jurisdiction. citeturn0search6

Electricity Consumers: Complaints and Rights

How to escalate an electricity complaint

NERC's published complaint procedure follows a staged process:

  1. Start with the DisCo: submit the complaint to the Customer Complaints Unit (CCU) of the electricity distribution company serving the customer.
  2. Escalate to the NERC Consumer Forum: if the complaint is not satisfactorily resolved or there is an applicable delay, the customer can refer it to the relevant Consumer Forum.
  3. Escalate to NERC: a customer dissatisfied with the Forum's ruling can escalate the matter to NERC through its published complaint channels and supporting documentation.

This process is important because NERC generally expects the DisCo's complaint mechanism to be used before the matter reaches the Forum. citeturn0search4turn0search3

Examples of published consumer rights

  • The right to safe and reliable electricity service.
  • The right to a properly installed and functional meter where applicable.
  • The right to transparent billing.
  • The right to contest an electricity bill.
  • The right to complain and have complaints investigated.
  • The right to escalate an unresolved complaint through the regulatory complaint process.

NERC also publishes consumer obligations, including paying for electricity consumed, complying with applicable connection requirements, and avoiding meter tampering or bypass. citeturn0search5

Licensing and Regulatory Approvals

Where NERC has jurisdiction, it issues or administers various licences, permits, certificates and authorisations for electricity-sector activities. NERC's published regulatory FAQ identifies examples including:

  • Generation licences
  • Transmission licences
  • Distribution licences for activities within NERC's jurisdiction
  • System Operation licences
  • Electricity Trading licences
  • Captive Power Generation permits
  • Meter Asset Provider certificates
  • Meter Service Provider certificates
  • Clearance for importation of generating sets

Requirements vary by licence or approval. Applicants should use the current NERC application forms, guidelines, fees and regulatory instruments rather than relying on a third-party summary. citeturn0search6

Tariffs and Market Regulation

NERC regulates applicable electricity tariffs and publishes regulatory orders and market instruments. The exact tariff applicable to a customer can depend on the regulatory framework, customer category, service conditions and current orders.

Because tariff classifications and regulatory orders can change, this site does not reproduce a tariff table that could quickly become outdated. Customers should verify the current tariff order and applicable customer classification through NERC and their electricity distribution company.

Information for Businesses and Investors

Businesses considering electricity generation, distribution, trading, embedded or other regulated activities should first establish which regulator has jurisdiction. Under the Electricity Act 2023, state-level regulation can apply to activities that are wholly within a state where the state has established the relevant regulatory authority, while NERC retains federal responsibilities described in its current guidance.

Applicants should check the applicable licence category, technical and financial requirements, incorporation and tax documents, project information, fees and processing requirements published by the relevant regulator.

Relationship to Other Government Institutions

NERC is a regulator rather than an electricity generator, transmission operator or distribution company. It regulates the activities of electricity licensees within its legal jurisdiction.

  • Federal Ministry of Power: responsible for broader federal power-sector policy functions; NERC performs independent regulatory functions under its governing legislation.
  • Transmission Company of Nigeria (TCN): operates transmission and system-related infrastructure subject to the applicable regulatory framework.
  • Electricity Distribution Companies (DisCos): provide distribution services to customers and operate customer complaint units that form the first step in NERC's published complaint process.
  • State electricity regulators: where a state has established regulatory authority under the Electricity Act 2023, the state regulator may have jurisdiction over specified intra-state electricity activities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is NERC an electricity company?

No. NERC is an electricity-sector regulator. Electricity generation, transmission and distribution are carried out by separate operators and licensees.

What law currently governs NERC?

NERC's current regulatory framework is based on the Electricity Act 2023, which replaced the Electric Power Sector Reform Act 2005.

Can I complain to NERC about my electricity bill?

Yes, but NERC's published process starts with the Customer Complaints Unit of the relevant DisCo. If the issue is not satisfactorily resolved, it can be escalated to the NERC Consumer Forum and then, where applicable, to NERC. citeturn0search4

Does NERC set electricity tariffs?

NERC regulates applicable electricity tariffs and publishes regulatory orders. The applicable tariff depends on the current regulatory framework and customer circumstances.

Can every electricity project apply to NERC?

Not necessarily. The Electricity Act 2023 allows state electricity regulation in specified circumstances. Applicants should determine whether NERC or a state regulator has jurisdiction before applying. citeturn0search6
