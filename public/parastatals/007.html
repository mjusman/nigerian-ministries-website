What Is NIMASA?

The Nigerian Maritime Administration and Safety Agency (NIMASA) is Nigeria's apex maritime regulatory and promotional agency. It was created in 2006 through the merger of the former National Maritime Authority and Joint Maritime Labour Industrial Council, and its statutory responsibilities are principally defined by the NIMASA Act 2007 together with other maritime legislation.

NIMASA's responsibilities cover maritime administration, shipping regulation, seafarer standards, maritime labour, safety and security, cabotage, commercial shipping development, search and rescue, and protection of the marine environment. It also implements relevant international maritime and labour conventions domesticated by Nigeria.

Legal and Institutional Basis

NIMASA identifies the following major legal instruments as part of the framework governing its functions:

  • NIMASA Act 2007 — establishes the Agency and sets out its statutory responsibilities.
  • Merchant Shipping Act 2007 — provides an important part of the legal framework for merchant shipping and maritime administration.
  • Coastal and Inland Shipping (Cabotage) Act 2003 — provides the statutory framework for cabotage and related Nigerian participation in coastal and inland shipping.
  • Suppression of Piracy and Other Maritime Offences (SPOMO) Act 2019 — forms part of Nigeria's maritime-security legal framework.

NIMASA also works with international maritime and labour conventions that apply to Nigeria. Specific legal questions should be checked against the current legislation and official guidance rather than this summary.

Core Functions

  • Administer and regulate shipping licences and related maritime activities.
  • Register ships and administer Nigerian flag-state responsibilities.
  • Regulate and certify seafarers and establish maritime training and safety standards.
  • Conduct surveys, inspections and certification related to ship safety and compliance.
  • Perform port-state control functions for applicable vessels.
  • Provide or coordinate maritime search and rescue services.
  • Support maritime security and vessel-security compliance.
  • Implement the Cabotage Act and administer related cabotage services.
  • Promote indigenous capacity in ship ownership, manning, construction and other maritime activities.
  • Prevent and control marine pollution and manage related environmental requirements.
  • Investigate marine casualties and incidents within its statutory responsibilities.
  • Carry out relevant surveillance, navigation, hydrographic and maritime-domain functions.

These functions are based on NIMASA's published statutory and institutional information. The Agency's precise role in an incident depends on the applicable law and the other institutions involved.

Services and Information Available

Ship registration and certification

NIMASA provides ship-registration services and publishes requirements, forms, tariffs and processing information for relevant applications. Its official site also provides information on ship surveys, tonnage measurement, construction and equipment standards and related certification.

Seafarer services

NIMASA provides information on seafarer registration, training and certification, including Certificate of Competency processes, seafarer identity documentation, medical examination and related professional requirements.

Cabotage services

The Agency administers aspects of Nigeria's cabotage regime, including information on cabotage vessel operations, waivers and the Cabotage Vessel Financing Fund (CVFF).

Maritime safety and security

Services and regulatory information include flag-state and port-state control, inspections, navigation systems, ship-port interface security, maritime incident investigation and search and rescue.

Marine environmental management

NIMASA publishes requirements concerning pollution prevention, marine environmental regulations, wreck removal, offshore waste reception facilities, ship recycling and related environmental compliance.

Licensing and permits

The Agency publishes procedures for areas including shipping company or agent registration, recognised organisations, recognised security organisations, stevedoring companies and guard-force companies.

Who Uses NIMASA Services?

  • Shipowners, ship operators and shipping companies.
  • Seafarers and maritime professionals.
  • Maritime training institutions and employers of seafarers.
  • Shipping agents and other maritime businesses.
  • Companies involved in cabotage and coastal or inland shipping.
  • Port, terminal and marine-service operators affected by maritime regulation.
  • Organisations with marine environmental compliance responsibilities.
  • Members of the public who need to report or obtain information about maritime incidents and regulatory matters.

Requirements vary substantially between services. NIMASA's official service pages should be used to determine the documents, fees, forms and processing steps for a particular application.

Certificate and Document Verification

NIMASA provides online verification facilities for certificates issued by the Agency. This can be useful for employers, seafarers and other organisations that need to confirm whether a maritime certificate is genuine.

Use NIMASA's official website to access the current certificate-verification service.

Search and Rescue and Maritime Incidents

NIMASA has a statutory role in maritime search and rescue and publishes information for maritime emergency response. If a maritime emergency is occurring, use NIMASA's official emergency/search-and-rescue channels rather than relying on a third-party directory.

For non-emergency complaints, feedback and service issues, NIMASA also provides a contact centre and online feedback channels.

Relationship With Other Maritime Institutions

NIMASA is part of a wider maritime system. Its official website identifies the Federal Ministry of Marine and Blue Economy as a partner and also lists institutions including the Nigerian Ports Authority (NPA), Nigerian Shippers' Council (NSC), Nigeria Customs Service (NCS), National Inland Waterways Authority (NIWA) and National Oil Spill Detection and Response Agency (NOSDRA).

These organisations have different mandates. For example, NIMASA regulates and administers important maritime functions, while NPA has responsibilities relating to port administration. A question about a port, customs clearance, inland waterways or an oil-spill response may therefore need to be directed to a different institution.

Service Standards and Complaints

NIMASA publishes a Service Charter describing service commitments, customer rights and responsibilities, and mechanisms for complaints where service delivery does not meet the stated standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does NIMASA regulate?

NIMASA regulates and administers many aspects of shipping, maritime safety, seafarer standards, maritime labour, cabotage, maritime security and marine environmental protection within its statutory mandate.

Does NIMASA register ships?

Yes. Ship registration is one of NIMASA's published services, alongside ship surveys and certification.

Does NIMASA certify seafarers?

Yes. The Agency publishes information on seafarer registration, training and certification, including Certificate of Competency processes.

What is cabotage?

In Nigeria's maritime framework, cabotage concerns regulated coastal and inland shipping activities. NIMASA administers relevant aspects of the Cabotage Act and publishes information on waivers, vessel operations and the CVFF.

Can I verify a NIMASA certificate?

Yes. NIMASA provides an online certificate-verification facility through its official website.

How do I report a maritime emergency?

Use NIMASA's official search-and-rescue and emergency-response channels. For current emergency contacts, check the Agency's official website because operational contact information can change.
