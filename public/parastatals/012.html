What Is the EFCC?

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) is a federal law-enforcement and investigative institution established by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (Establishment) Act 2004. Its statutory responsibilities include the investigation and enforcement of laws relating to economic and financial crimes.

The EFCC's published mandate covers investigation of financial crimes, coordination and enforcement of relevant economic and financial-crime laws, tracing and recovery of proceeds of crime, prevention and public enlightenment, and cooperation with other authorities in Nigeria and abroad.

Important: Nigerian Info is an independent educational website. It is not the EFCC and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Commission. For petitions, investigations, official notices, auctions, SCUML matters or other current procedures, use the official EFCC channels linked below.

Legal and Institutional Basis

The EFCC was established under the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (Establishment) Act 2004. The Act assigns the Commission functions including enforcement and administration of the Act, investigation of economic and financial crimes, coordination of relevant enforcement functions, and measures connected with tracing, freezing, confiscating and recovering proceeds of crime.

The Commission also operates within Nigeria's wider legal framework for financial crime, money laundering and proceeds-of-crime recovery. The applicable law depends on the particular matter, so current legislation and official guidance should be checked before relying on any legal interpretation.

Core Functions

  • Investigating economic and financial crimes within its statutory jurisdiction.
  • Enforcing and administering the EFCC Establishment Act and relevant economic and financial-crime laws.
  • Tracing, identifying, freezing, seizing or recovering proceeds connected with offences, subject to applicable law and judicial or other legal processes.
  • Coordinating or collaborating with other Nigerian authorities on economic and financial-crime enforcement.
  • Working with international law-enforcement and financial-crime bodies on matters within its mandate.
  • Undertaking prevention, education and public-enlightenment activities concerning economic and financial crimes.
  • Supporting intelligence-led investigation and enforcement against financial crime.

The Commission's official establishment material and public FAQ provide the primary basis for this summary.

Public Services and Official Channels

Submitting a petition

The EFCC provides an official channel for submitting petitions concerning matters that may fall within its mandate. The Commission's current website also provides a petition-tracking option. Use the official site rather than an unofficial intermediary.

Tracking a petition

Where an official petition has been submitted through the EFCC's process, the Commission provides a tracking facility through its website. Keep any reference information supplied during submission.

Reporting suspected misconduct by EFCC staff

The official EFCC website provides a channel for reporting staff-related concerns. Use the Commission's current official procedure and retain relevant details of the complaint.

SCUML

The Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering (SCUML) supervises and regulates designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs) for anti-money-laundering and counter-financing-of-terrorism compliance. Its official services include registration and certification, compliance supervision and reporting-related functions. SCUML is operationally domiciled within the EFCC while its statutory positioning is described by its official site in relation to the Federal Ministry of Industry, Trade and Investment.

Other EFCC online services

The official EFCC website currently provides links for petitions, petition tracking, staff reports, auctions, SCUML applications and other public information. Availability and procedures should be confirmed directly on the official site.

Who Uses EFCC Services and Information?

  • Members of the public: to submit information or petitions concerning suspected economic and financial crimes.
  • Businesses and organisations: where financial-crime matters or applicable compliance obligations fall within the Commission's remit.
  • DNFBPs: through SCUML for applicable AML/CFT registration, supervision and compliance requirements.
  • Government institutions and law-enforcement bodies: for inter-agency coordination and investigations within applicable mandates.
  • International partners: where cooperation is required for cross-border financial-crime investigations or asset recovery.

Important Information Before Submitting a Petition or Report

  • Use the EFCC's official website and current submission process.
  • Provide factual information and relevant supporting documents where available.
  • Keep copies of documents and any reference number generated by the official system.
  • Do not submit passwords, banking PINs, one-time passwords or other authentication secrets as part of an ordinary complaint.
  • Do not assume that submitting a petition establishes that an allegation is true; investigation and, where applicable, judicial processes determine the outcome.
  • For legal advice about a particular dispute or suspected offence, consult a qualified legal professional rather than relying on this explanatory page.

Relationship to Other Government Institutions

The EFCC operates within Nigeria's wider law-enforcement, justice and financial-governance system. Its work can involve cooperation with other Nigerian institutions and international partners, but those institutions retain their own statutory mandates.

  • Nigeria Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU): a separate institution responsible for financial intelligence functions.
  • ICPC: the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission has a separate statutory anti-corruption mandate.
  • Police and other law-enforcement bodies: may have overlapping or complementary investigative responsibilities under their respective laws.
  • SCUML: an EFCC-domiciled unit dealing with AML/CFT supervision of DNFBPs as described on its official website.

These institutions should not be treated as interchangeable. The appropriate institution depends on the facts and legal jurisdiction of the matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

When was the EFCC established?

The EFCC was established under the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (Establishment) Act 2004.

What kinds of matters does the EFCC investigate?

The EFCC Establishment Act includes financial crimes such as advance-fee fraud, money laundering, fraudulent transfers, contract scams and other economic and financial crimes within the Commission's legal mandate.

Can a member of the public submit a petition?

Yes. The EFCC provides an official petition-submission channel. Use the current procedure on the Commission's website.

Does the EFCC decide whether someone is guilty?

No government information page should treat an allegation as a finding of guilt. The EFCC investigates and prosecutes matters within its mandate; criminal guilt is determined through the applicable judicial process.

What is SCUML?

SCUML is the Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering. It supervises and regulates applicable DNFBPs for AML/CFT compliance and provides registration, certification and compliance services.

Where can I verify current EFCC procedures?

Use the official EFCC website and its current service portals. Nigerian Info provides explanatory context and is not an official EFCC channel.
