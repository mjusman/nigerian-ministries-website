About the Nigerian Ports Authority (NPA)

The Nigerian Ports Authority (NPA) is a Federal Government agency responsible for managing, operating, and developing Nigeria's seaports. It was established by the Nigerian Ports Authority Act to provide efficient port services, facilitate maritime trade, and promote economic development through modern port infrastructure and operations.

The Authority plays a critical role in supporting Nigeria's import and export activities by ensuring that vessels, cargo, and passengers move safely and efficiently through the nation's ports.

Core Functions

  • Manage and maintain Nigeria's seaport infrastructure.
  • Provide safe navigation channels for ships.
  • Develop and modernize port facilities.
  • Ensure efficient cargo handling operations.
  • Promote international maritime trade.
  • Coordinate port security and safety measures.
  • Provide marine services such as pilotage and towage.
  • Regulate port operations in collaboration with relevant agencies.
  • Support private sector participation in port management.

Objectives

  • Improve port efficiency and competitiveness.
  • Facilitate international commerce.
  • Reduce vessel turnaround time.
  • Enhance maritime safety and environmental protection.
  • Develop world-class port infrastructure.
  • Increase revenue generation for the Federal Government.
  • Support Nigeria's economic diversification through maritime trade.

Services Provided

  • Marine and harbour services
  • Pilotage services
  • Towage services
  • Berthing services
  • Cargo handling support
  • Port infrastructure maintenance
  • Port development projects
  • Port concession management
  • Shipping traffic management

Importance of the NPA

The Nigerian Ports Authority is one of the most important institutions supporting Nigeria's economy. Over 80% of the country's international trade by volume passes through Nigerian seaports. Efficient port operations help reduce the cost of doing business, improve trade competitiveness, create employment opportunities, and strengthen Nigeria's position as a major maritime hub in West Africa.
